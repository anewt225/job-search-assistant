# Resume Scoring Rubric

## Guiding Principles: The Three Commandments (Schifeling — Career Coach GPT)

Every scoring dimension connects back to these three principles:
1. **Make the Recruiter's Job Easy** — Clear structure, scannable layout, obvious fit
2. **Design Your Resume for Technology (ATS)** — Keywords, formatting, section headers
3. **Become the Obvious Choice** — Quantified achievements, JD-mirrored language, "yes statements"

## Scoring Instructions

Score each of the 9 dimensions on a 0-10 scale. Then compute the weighted composite.
For each dimension, provide: score, what's working (strengths), what needs work (gaps),
and specific recommendations.

Be honest and calibrated. A 7/10 means "good with room for improvement." A 10/10 means
"could not meaningfully improve this section." Most tailored resumes should land 70-85.
Scores above 90 are exceptional.

## PART 1: RESUME SECTION SCORES

### 1. Contact Information & Header (Weight: 10%)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 0 | No name or contact info visible |
| 3 | Name present but missing email, phone, or LinkedIn |
| 5 | All basics present but formatting inconsistent or ATS-unfriendly |
| 7 | Full contact block: name, email, phone, LinkedIn, city/state. Clean formatting |
| 9 | Above + portfolio/personal site if relevant. Visually prominent. Location matches JD |
| 10 | Perfect: all info present, ATS-parseable, visually balanced, location signals match |

**Check for**: Name prominence, email format (professional), phone number, LinkedIn URL,
city/state (especially for remote roles — shows timezone/US-based), optional portfolio link.

### 2. Summary / Profile (Weight: 12%)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 0 | No summary section |
| 3 | Generic summary with buzzwords, no JD-specific language |
| 5 | Mentions relevant experience but doesn't mirror JD terminology |
| 7 | Mirrors 3+ JD keywords, quantified snapshot, clear value proposition |
| 9 | Top Third Rule satisfied: hiring manager immediately sees fit. Industry + role + impact clear |
| 10 | Perfect: concise (40-55 words), JD-mirrored, quantified, no first person, includes 5+ critical keywords |

**Check for**: JD keyword density, quantification, length (40-55 words ideal), first vs.
third person, presence of critical terms from JD (e.g., "adult learning" if the JD says it).

**Top Third Rule**: This section is the most important for first-pass screening. It must
make the recruiter say "yes" within the first 1.2 seconds of their 6-second scan.

### 3. Professional Experience (Weight: 25%)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 0 | No experience section or completely irrelevant entries |
| 3 | Jobs listed but bullets describe responsibilities only, no achievements |
| 5 | Mix of responsibilities and accomplishments. Some quantification. Partial JD alignment |
| 7 | Majority accomplishment-driven bullets. 60%+ quantification rate. Clear JD mapping. 3 Ns present |
| 9 | Every bullet = accomplishment. 80%+ quantification. Strong 3 Ns. Last 2 roles deeply aligned to JD |
| 10 | Perfect: 90%+ quantification, every bullet is a "yes statement," narrative arc from past→present maps to target role, CAR structure throughout |

**Check for**:
- **Quantification rate**: Count bullets with numbers / total bullets. Target: 70%+
- **3 Ns coverage**: Numbers, Names, Notables in each bullet
- **Accomplishment vs. responsibility ratio**: Accomplishments describe impact; responsibilities describe duties
- **JD task mapping**: For each JD responsibility, is there at least one bullet that maps to it?
- **Recency weighting**: Last 2 roles get disproportionate attention (GLORY: "L" step)
- **Action verbs**: Strong, specific openers (achieved, designed, facilitated, led) vs. weak (assisted, helped, participated)
- **Bullet density**: 20-30 words per bullet for scannability. Flag any over 40 words
- **Story arc** (GLORY: "O" step): Is there a coherent narrative from past experience → target role?

**Top Third Rule check** (Schifeling):
Recruiters decide fit from the top third of page one. Check specifically:
- Do the Summary + most recent role carry 70%+ of the critical JD keywords?
- Are the strongest quantified accomplishments in the first 2 roles (not buried lower)?
- If a recruiter only reads the top third, would they say "yes, call this person"?

**Resume Tier check** (Dalton):
- Basic: bullets describe responsibilities → score ≤ 5
- Good: bullets describe major projects with some results → score 6-7
- Great: bullets describe impact + root causes with always-quantified results → score 8+

### 4. Education (Weight: 5%)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 0 | No education section |
| 3 | Education listed but formatting inconsistent or hard to parse |
| 5 | Standard format, all relevant credentials listed |
| 7 | Clean formatting, relevant credentials, any honors/distinctions noted |
| 9 | ATS-standard format (institution, degree, dates on separate lines), relevant to JD |
| 10 | Perfect formatting + strategic emphasis (e.g., relevant coursework if applicable) |

**Check for**: Consistent formatting, ATS-parseable structure, relevance to JD,
GPA/honors if impressive, distracting credentials de-emphasized.

### 5. Certifications (Weight: 5%)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 0 | No certifications and JD doesn't require any |
| 3 | Certifications present but buried inline or poorly formatted |
| 5 | Dedicated section with relevant certs listed |
| 7 | Well-formatted, relevant to JD, dates included |
| 9 | Certifications directly match JD requirements, well-organized |
| 10 | Perfect: JD-required certs prominent, supplementary certs support narrative |

**Check for**: Dedicated section header (ATS needs it), relevance to JD, formatting
consistency, whether missing certs the JD calls for.

### 6. Skills / Additional Information (Weight: 8%)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 0 | No skills section despite JD requiring specific technical skills |
| 3 | Generic skills list not aligned to JD |
| 5 | Some JD-relevant skills listed, missing critical ones |
| 7 | Dedicated Skills section with 70%+ JD keyword coverage. Additional info adds personality |
| 9 | Skills section mirrors JD language precisely. Additional info supports candidacy (board roles, affiliations) |
| 10 | Perfect: ATS keyword capture maximized, skills organized by category, additional info creates "easy-to-defend" signal |

**Check for**: Dedicated "Skills" or "Technical Skills" section header (ATS heavily
weights this), keyword coverage of JD hard skills, organization (by category vs. dump list),
languages if relevant, board memberships / affiliations that signal culture fit.

### 7. Formatting & ATS Compatibility (Weight: 15%)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 0 | Multi-column layout, graphics, or unreadable by ATS |
| 3 | ATS-parseable but significant formatting issues |
| 5 | Single column, standard font, .docx, but inconsistent heading styles or spacing |
| 7 | Clean ATS-friendly layout. Standard section headers. Consistent heading hierarchy |
| 9 | Professional formatting throughout. Proper margins (0.5"+). All sections properly headed |
| 10 | Perfect: ATS-optimized, consistent styling, proper heading hierarchy, dates right-aligned, clean whitespace |

**Check for**: Single-column layout, standard font (Calibri, Arial, Garamond), .docx format,
standard section headers ("Professional Experience" not creative alternatives), heading
hierarchy (H1→H2→body), margins ≥ 0.5", no tables/graphics that break ATS parsing,
consistent bullet style, no orphaned formatting.

### 8. White Space & Visual Balance (Weight: 5%)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 0 | Walls of text with no visual breaks |
| 3 | Some structure but cramped or uneven spacing |
| 5 | Adequate whitespace but inconsistent spacing between sections |
| 7 | Good visual hierarchy. Clear section breaks. Readable at a glance |
| 9 | Professional visual balance. Company names visually distinct. Breathing room between sections |
| 10 | Perfect: could pass as professionally designed. Balanced density. Invites the eye |

**Check for**: Top-of-page impression (does it look complete?), margin adequacy,
section separation, visual hierarchy between company names / titles / bullets,
overall density (too dense = hard to scan in 6 seconds).

### 9. GLORY Score (Weight: 15%)

This is a holistic assessment of how well the resume applies the GLORY framework from
Reverse the Search (Mann).

| Element | 0-2 (Weak) | 3-5 (Developing) | 6-8 (Strong) | 9-10 (Exceptional) |
|---------|-----------|------------------|-------------|-------------------|
| **G** Gather Keywords | <30% JD keyword match | 30-50% match | 50-75% match | 75%+ match with natural integration |
| **L** List Tasks | No task mapping to JD | Some tasks map, gaps in key areas | Most JD responsibilities covered | Every JD responsibility has a matching bullet |
| **O** Observe Story | No narrative arc | Partial arc, career shifts unexplained | Clear progression toward target role | Compelling story: "of course this person should have this job" |
| **R** Refine Accomplishments | Responsibilities only | Mix of responsibilities and accomplishments | Mostly accomplishments with quantification | Every bullet is a refined, quantified accomplishment |
| **Y** Yes Statements | Hiring manager would say "maybe" | Some "yes" signals but gaps | Strong "yes" on most JD requirements | Unambiguous "yes" on every critical requirement |

Score each element 0-10, then average for the GLORY dimension score.

## PART 2: JD ALIGNMENT SCORING

### Requirements Match

For each requirement listed in the JD (qualifications, years of experience, degrees,
certifications, skills):

- **10/10**: Exceeds requirement with clear evidence
- **7/10**: Meets requirement
- **5/10**: Partially meets, transferable evidence
- **3/10**: Weak match, stretch claim
- **0/10**: Not addressed at all

Calculate: Sum of scores / (count × 10) × 100 = Requirements Match %

### Responsibilities Match

For each responsibility listed in the JD (what you'll do day-to-day):

- **10/10**: Direct, quantified experience doing exactly this
- **7/10**: Similar experience, clearly transferable
- **5/10**: Related experience, would need some explanation
- **3/10**: Tangential experience
- **0/10**: No evidence

Calculate: Sum of scores / (count × 10) × 100 = Responsibilities Match %

### Keyword Match

Count the number of distinct JD keywords/phrases found in the resume vs. total
JD keywords identified in Step 2.

Calculate: Keywords found / Total JD keywords × 100 = Keyword Match %

### JD Fit Average

Average of Requirements Match %, Responsibilities Match %, and Keyword Match %.

## PART 3: KEYWORD GAP ANALYSIS

List every critical JD keyword NOT found in the resume. Categorize by severity using
this taxonomy:

- **Critical Missing** (appears 2+ times in JD OR listed in minimum requirements OR used
  in the role title): Must add immediately. These keywords are what ATS filters scan for
  first. Missing even one can mean automatic rejection.
- **Important Missing** (appears once in responsibilities section or in "what you'll do"):
  Should add. These won't cause automatic rejection but their absence weakens the "yes
  statement" signal for the hiring manager.
- **Nice-to-Have Missing** (appears in preferred/bonus/nice-to-have section only): Consider
  adding if they can be integrated naturally. Don't force them — forced keywords read as
  dishonest and can backfire in interviews.

For each missing keyword, note:
1. The exact phrase from the JD
2. Where it appears in the JD (requirements, responsibilities, or preferred)
3. How many times it appears
4. A suggested placement in the resume (which section/bullet to add it to)

## PART 4: 50% RULE ASSESSMENT

The 50% Rule (Schifeling — Career Coach GPT) states that candidates meeting ~50% of job
requirements have the best interview conversion rate. Over-qualification can actually
decrease your chances. This section translates the Requirements Match into a clear
go/no-go recommendation.

Based on the Requirements Match:

- State clearly: "You meet approximately X% of the stated requirements"
- Then apply this decision logic:

| Requirements Match | Verdict | Recommended Action |
|-------------------|---------|-------------------|
| **≥70%** | Strong match — possibly overqualified | Submit confidently. Consider whether you should be targeting more senior roles. |
| **50-69%** | Sweet spot — optimal zone per 50% Rule | Submit with confidence. This is where interview conversion is highest. Focus on tailoring the resume to close remaining keyword gaps. |
| **40-49%** | Borderline — viable with support | Pursue ONLY with a warm referral, strong cover letter, or consultative approach (Mann). Without at least one of these, your resume will likely be filtered out by ATS or recruiter screening. |
| **30-39%** | Stretch — significant gap | Reconsider this specific role. Either: (a) find a more aligned posting at the same company, (b) use this as a networking target rather than an application target, or (c) invest heavily in a referral who can vouch for your transferable skills. |
| **<30%** | Misaligned | Do not apply to this specific posting. The gap is too large to bridge with tailoring alone. Look for roles at this company that better match your background, or use this company as a long-term networking target. |

## PART 5: COMPOSITE SCORE CALCULATION

| Component | Weight |
|-----------|--------|
| Contact Information | 10% |
| Summary | 12% |
| Professional Experience | 25% |
| Education | 5% |
| Certifications | 5% |
| Skills / Additional Info | 8% |
| Formatting & ATS | 15% |
| White Space & Visual | 5% |
| GLORY Score | 15% |

**Resume Quality Score** = Σ (dimension score × weight) × 10

**JD Fit Score** = JD Fit Average (from Part 2)

**Composite Score** = (Resume Quality × 40%) + (JD Fit Score × 60%)

The 60/40 weighting toward JD Fit reflects the reality that a beautifully formatted
resume that doesn't match the JD will still get rejected. Alignment > aesthetics.

---

## PART 6: PRIORITY ACTION ITEMS

Organize recommendations into:

### Immediate (Do Before Submitting)
Items that would cause automatic rejection or major red flags. Examples: missing contact
info, critical keyword gaps, formatting that breaks ATS.

### High Priority (Strengthen Fit)
Items that meaningfully improve the score. Examples: reframing bullets to mirror JD
language, adding missing skills section, quantifying key accomplishments.

### Nice to Have (Polish)
Items that improve from good to great. Examples: tightening bullet length, adding a
notable name, improving visual balance.

---

## PART 7: EASY-TO-DEFEND ASSESSMENT

Based on the "Easy-to-Defend Candidate" framework (Dalton — The Job Closer), assess:

1. **Has done this job before?** (strongest signal)
2. **Elite employer on resume?** (credibility signal)
3. **Elite school on resume?** (credibility signal)
4. **Clear, scannable evidence in 6 seconds?** (recruiter risk reduction)

State the "defense case" — what a recruiter would say to justify advancing this candidate
to the hiring manager. If the defense case is weak, say so and recommend how to strengthen it.
