# Negotiation Playbook

Principles (Mann, Schifeling, Terry):
1. **Gratitude first.** Enthusiasm is leverage; they need to believe you'll sign.
2. **No pressure, no on-the-spot answers.** "Can I have until <day> to review?" is standard.
3. **Direct and brief.** State the anchor once, with reasons, then stop talking.
4. **Auction dynamics.** Multiple processes that know about each other create urgency. Keep every
   process informed.
5. **Negotiate the whole package, not just base.** Use the four legs: comp · budget · resources ·
   support. A job mission with OKRs (Terry) justifies asking for resources.
6. **One negotiation round is normal; two is the usual maximum.** Bundle your asks; don't drip them.

## Scripts
**Expectations, asked early**
"I'm focused on finding the right fit, and I'm confident we'll land on something fair if we get there.
What range is budgeted for the role?" If pressed: "Based on my research for this scope in <location>,
roles like this land around <p50–p75>."

**Verbal offer**
"Thank you, I'm really excited about this. Could you send the full details in writing? I'd like to take
until <day> to review everything and come back to you."

**Counter (email)**
```
Hi <Name>,
Thank you again for the offer. I'm excited about <specific thing about the team/mission>.
Having reviewed the full package, and given <market data for this scope in <location> / a competing offer at <level> / the <specific value> I'll bring in <area>>, I'd like to ask whether there's flexibility to bring the base to <target>. <Optional: If base is fixed, a sign-on of <X> or <lever> would help close the gap.>
If we can get there, I'm ready to move forward.
Best, <Name>
```

**Competing offer / acceleration**
"I want to be transparent: I've received an offer from another company with a decision deadline of
<date>. <Company> is my first choice. Is there any way to accelerate the remaining steps, or to share
where the package might land?"

**"This is our best offer"**
"I understand. Is there flexibility on <sign-on / equity / start date / review at 6 months with a
defined increase>?" Then decide against your walk-away, not against your nerves.

**Extension**
"I want to give this the consideration it deserves. Could I have until <date>? I'll come back to you
sooner if I can."

**Decline, keeping the door open**
"Thank you for the offer and for the time your team invested. After a lot of thought I've decided to
accept another role that's a closer fit for <reason>. I've really enjoyed getting to know <names> and
would love to stay in touch."

**Accept**
Confirm in writing: title, base, bonus target, equity (amount and vesting), sign-on (and any clawback),
start date, remote or location policy, and anything agreed verbally.

## Non-salary levers
Sign-on bonus · equity/RSUs · title · start date · remote or hybrid days · PTO · an earlier first review
(e.g. at 6 months) · a learning or certification budget · relocation · equipment or home office · a
defined scope or team (resources).
