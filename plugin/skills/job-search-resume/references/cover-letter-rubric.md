# Cover Letter Scoring Rubric

## Scoring Instructions

Score each of the 7 dimensions on a 0-10 scale. Then compute the weighted composite.
For each dimension, provide: score, what's working (strengths), what needs work (gaps),
and specific recommendations.

Cover letters serve a different function than resumes. Only 26% of recruiters claim to
read them (Linked), but for the ones who do, a strong cover letter can be a tiebreaker.
The Pareto Cover Letter framework (Schifeling) says: 80% strong base structure, 20%
company-specific customization. 250-350 words max.

---

## PART 1: COVER LETTER SECTION SCORES

### 1. Opening Hook (Weight: 20%)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 0 | Generic opening ("I am writing to apply for...") |
| 3 | Mentions the company/role but no specific hook |
| 5 | References something about the company but it's surface-level (from the careers page) |
| 7 | Specific, recent company reference (news, product launch, earnings call, cultural initiative) |
| 9 | Compelling hook that connects something specific about the company to the candidate's passion/experience |
| 10 | Perfect: irresistible opening that makes the reader want to keep going. Demonstrates genuine knowledge and enthusiasm. Names a referral if applicable |

**Check for**: Is the first sentence specific to THIS company (not copy-pasteable)?
Does it reference something recent and concrete? Does it name a referral contact if one exists?
Does it establish enthusiasm immediately?

**Pareto principle**: The opening is the 20% customization that matters most.

### 2. Keyword Integration (Weight: 15%)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 0 | No JD keywords present |
| 3 | A few keywords scattered but feel forced |
| 5 | Keywords present but concentrated in one paragraph |
| 7 | 5+ JD keywords woven naturally throughout |
| 9 | Keywords integrated so naturally they read as the candidate's own vocabulary |
| 10 | Perfect: high keyword density that still reads as authentic, conversational prose |

**Check for**: Count distinct JD keywords/phrases. Assess whether they feel natural or
stuffed. Keywords should be distributed across paragraphs, not clustered. The goal is
ATS keyword capture in narrative form.

### 3. Evidence & Accomplishments (Weight: 20%)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 0 | No specific examples or accomplishments |
| 3 | Vague references to experience ("I have extensive experience in...") |
| 5 | 1-2 specific examples but not quantified or not mapped to JD requirements |
| 7 | 2-3 specific, quantified accomplishments clearly mapped to JD requirements |
| 9 | Each body paragraph anchored by a compelling, quantified accomplishment that directly answers a JD need |
| 10 | Perfect: accomplishments selected strategically to cover the 2-3 most critical JD requirements, quantified with 3 Ns, and tell a coherent story |

**Check for**: Are accomplishments selected for relevance to this JD (not just impressive
in general)? Are they quantified (Numbers, Names, Notables)? Do they map to specific JD
requirements or responsibilities? The cover letter should answer the JD's top 2-3 needs,
not try to cover everything.

### 4. Narrative & Story Arc (Weight: 15%)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 0 | No narrative structure — reads like a list of qualifications |
| 3 | Paragraphs exist but don't connect to each other |
| 5 | Basic structure (intro → experience → closing) but no compelling thread |
| 7 | Clear narrative arc: why this candidate + why this company + why now |
| 9 | Compelling story that makes the reader understand the candidate's journey and why this role is a natural next step |
| 10 | Perfect: the cover letter tells a story that only THIS candidate could tell about why THEY are right for THIS role at THIS company |

**Check for**: Does the letter flow as a narrative or read as disconnected paragraphs?
Is there a "why now" signal? Does the GLORY "O" (Observe Story) arc come through —
a coherent journey from past experience toward this specific role? Does it avoid simply
restating the resume in paragraph form?

### 5. Tone & Voice (Weight: 10%)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 0 | Robotic, formulaic, or clearly AI-generated |
| 3 | Professional but generic — could be anyone |
| 5 | Appropriate tone but no personality |
| 7 | Professional with genuine personality. Sounds like a real person |
| 9 | Authentic voice that conveys both competence and warmth (Schifeling's "Competence + Warmth" formula) |
| 10 | Perfect: distinctive voice, appropriate enthusiasm, reads like the candidate is having a conversation with the hiring manager |

**Check for**: Does it sound like a human wrote it? Is there a balance of competence
(credentials, achievements) and warmth (enthusiasm, personality, cultural fit)? Does the
tone match the company culture (e.g., more casual for a startup, more formal for consulting)?
Are there any "AI tells" (overly structured, buzzword-heavy, no contractions)?

### 6. Structure & Length (Weight: 10%)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 0 | No clear structure or wildly inappropriate length |
| 3 | Too long (>400 words) or too short (<150 words) |
| 5 | Reasonable length but paragraphs are unbalanced or structure is unclear |
| 7 | 250-350 words, 3-4 clear paragraphs, logical flow |
| 9 | Pareto structure: opening hook → 2 body paragraphs mapping to JD → confident close |
| 10 | Perfect: tight, scannable, every sentence earns its place, Pareto structure executed flawlessly |

**Check for**: Word count (250-350 ideal per Schifeling). Paragraph count (3-4 ideal).
Balance between paragraphs. Does every sentence serve a purpose? Is the closing confident
with a clear call to action?

**Pareto structure**:
- P1: Hook + referral name + enthusiasm signal (20% customization)
- P2-P3: 2 body paragraphs, each anchored by an accomplishment that maps to a top JD need (80% base)
- P4: Confident close with forward-looking statement and call to action

### 7. Closing & Call to Action (Weight: 10%)

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 0 | No closing or abrupt ending |
| 3 | Generic closing ("Thank you for your consideration") |
| 5 | Polite close but no forward-looking language |
| 7 | Confident close that expresses enthusiasm and suggests next steps |
| 9 | Closing reinforces the unique value proposition and creates a sense of momentum |
| 10 | Perfect: memorable close that leaves the reader wanting to meet this candidate, with a specific and confident call to action |

**Check for**: Does the close reinforce the candidate's value proposition? Is there
a forward-looking statement (not just backward-looking gratitude)? Does it express
genuine enthusiasm without being needy? Is there a specific call to action?

## PART 2: JD ALIGNMENT SCORING

### Keyword Match

Count the number of distinct JD keywords/phrases found in the cover letter vs.
total JD keywords identified.

Calculate: Keywords found / Total JD keywords × 100 = Keyword Match %

Note: Cover letters should capture 30-50% of JD keywords (lower threshold than resumes
because the format is narrative, not keyword-optimized).

### Requirements Addressed

How many of the JD's top requirements does the cover letter specifically address with
evidence?

- Count JD requirements explicitly addressed with accomplishment evidence
- Calculate: Addressed / Total Requirements × 100

Target: Address the top 2-3 requirements, not all of them. Quality > coverage.

### Enthusiasm Signal

Does the cover letter clearly convey genuine interest in:
- This specific company (not generic industry interest)
- This specific role (not a general career aspiration)
- The intersection of both (why THIS role at THIS company)

Score: 0 (no enthusiasm), 5 (generic enthusiasm), 10 (specific, credible enthusiasm)

### JD Alignment Average

Average of Keyword Match %, Requirements Addressed %, and Enthusiasm Signal (×10 to normalize).

---

## PART 3: KEYWORD GAP ANALYSIS

List critical JD keywords NOT found in the cover letter. Categorize as:

- **Should Include** (top 3-5 JD keywords that are missing): These should definitely
  appear somewhere in the narrative
- **Consider Including** (secondary keywords): Nice to have but don't force them
- **Skip** (keywords better left to the resume): Technical skills, certifications,
  and specific tools that don't fit narrative prose

---

## PART 4: COMPOSITE SCORE CALCULATION

| Component | Weight |
|-----------|--------|
| Opening Hook | 20% |
| Keyword Integration | 15% |
| Evidence & Accomplishments | 20% |
| Narrative & Story Arc | 15% |
| Tone & Voice | 10% |
| Structure & Length | 10% |
| Closing & Call to Action | 10% |

**Cover Letter Quality Score** = Σ (dimension score × weight) × 10

**JD Alignment Score** = JD Alignment Average (from Part 2)

**Composite Score** = (Cover Letter Quality × 50%) + (JD Alignment Score × 50%)

The 50/50 weighting reflects the dual purpose of cover letters: they must be well-crafted
prose AND clearly aligned to the opportunity.

---

## PART 5: PRIORITY ACTION ITEMS

### Immediate (Do Before Submitting)
Critical issues: generic opening, no company-specific content, missing accomplishments,
wrong tone for the audience.

### High Priority (Strengthen Impact)
Items that meaningfully improve the score: adding a specific recent company hook,
quantifying accomplishments, tightening to Pareto structure, naming the referral.

### Nice to Have (Polish)
Items that improve from good to great: tightening word count, improving the close,
adding one more JD keyword naturally.

---

## PART 6: COMPETENCE + WARMTH ASSESSMENT

Based on Schifeling's framework from Career Coach GPT:

**Competence signals** (does the reader believe this person can do the job?):
- Quantified accomplishments
- Relevant experience citations
- Technical/domain vocabulary
- Clear understanding of the role's challenges

**Warmth signals** (does the reader want to work with this person?):
- Genuine enthusiasm (not performative)
- Personality that comes through
- Cultural fit indicators
- Collaborative language

Score each 0-10, then note which is stronger. Ideally both are ≥7. A high-competence
but low-warmth letter gets filed as "qualified but..." A high-warmth but low-competence
letter gets filed as "seems great but can they do the job?"
