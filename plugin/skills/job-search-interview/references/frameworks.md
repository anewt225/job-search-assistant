# Interview Frameworks (condensed)

Sources: Dalton, *The Job Closer* (JC) · Mann, *Reverse the Search* (RtS) · Schifeling, *Career Coach GPT*
(CCG) · Dalton, *The 2-Hour Job Search* (2HJS).

## The Big Four (JC): about 60% of the decision is made in the first 15 minutes
| When | What happens | Share of decision |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 min | Small talk / first impression | ~5% |
| 1–5 min | Tell me about yourself (TMAY) | ~25% |
| 5–15 min | Why this job / org / sector | ~30% |
| 15+ min | Behavioural, case, Q&A | ~18% |
| After | Follow-up, thank-you | ~22% |
These are predictable, so over-prepare them.

## FIT, for TMAY and "walk me through your resume" (JC)
One orienting sentence → for each stage, the **F**avourite part ("favourite" beats "passionate") →
**I**nsight ("*but* I realised…") → **T**ransition ("*therefore* I…") → finish by teasing interest in this
role, then stop. Keep it under 2 minutes with 3 beats at most. Link the beats with "but" and "therefore",
not "and then".

## RAC, for "why us / why this role" (JC)
3 **R**easons → an **A**necdote for each that is authentic, specific and informed (ideally from an
informational chat) → a **C**onnection back to what you bring.

## CAR, for behavioural questions (CCG, RtS, JC)
| Part | What it covers | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Challenge | The problem, in 1–2 sentences | ~15 s |
| Action | What *you* did, with your decisions and reasoning ("I", not "we") | ~90 s |
| Result | A quantified outcome, ideally with one of the 3 Ns (Numbers, Names, Notables) | ~15 s |
Keep the whole answer under 2 minutes. **SCAR** handles failure and weakness questions: a **S**etback from an
earlier experience, then C-A-R showing what you did differently.
**Story toolbox** (RtS): 10–15 CAR stories, with a Top 3 that flex across leadership, ambiguity and
cross-functional work. **Guidepost phrases** (JC) are the only lines worth memorising word for word.

## Competence + warmth (CCG)
Interviewers judge two things: *can they do the job* (CAR covers this) and *do I want to work with them*.
Warmth shows up as answers under 2 minutes, 130–160 words per minute, varied pitch, few fillers, and more
energy than feels natural on video. **The likability test** (RtS): interviewers decide whether they like you,
then rationalise it.

## Consultative approach (RtS)
Treat the interview as a consulting engagement. Research the "client": site, news from the last 3–6
months, competitors, the product. Name their likely pain points and bring a point of view on solving
them. In later rounds, bring a **90-day plan**: learn (days 1–30), build (31–60), deliver (61–90).

## Small talk (JC)
Confirmation bias starts at hello. Answer temperature-check questions with a real detail, "yes, and"
what they offer, and listen more than you talk.

## Questions to ask
Use TIARA (Trends, Insights, Advice, Resources, Assignments) for rapport, plus consultative questions:
- "What does success look like at 30, 60 and 90 days?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"
- "I noticed [initiative]. How does this role connect to it?"
- "Which metrics does this team own, and how are they trending?"

Always close with **"What are the next steps, and when should I expect to hear back?"** Ask at least 3
questions in total.

## Stage-specific emphasis
| Stage | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| Recruiter or phone screen | TMAY ×3 variants, 5 likely questions, comp range ready (deflect to "market rate for the role" if early), logistics |
| Hiring manager | Consultative deep dive, 8–10 CAR answers mapped to JD requirements, warmth |
| Case / technical | Structure first: state the framework, then size or prioritise, then recommend. Bring domain examples |
| Panel / final | Executive framing, the "why us" story, 90-day plan, questions that evaluate mutual fit, negotiation readiness |
