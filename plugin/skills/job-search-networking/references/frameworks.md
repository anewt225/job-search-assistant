# Networking Frameworks (condensed)

Sources: Dalton *The 2-Hour Job Search* (2HJS) · Terry *Never Search Alone* (NSA) · Mann *Reverse the
Search* (RtS) · Garriott & Schifeling *Linked* · Hoffman & Casnocha *The Start-Up of You*.

## Core model
1. **Conversations are the output; messages are the input.** A week of 20 messages and 0 calls failed.
2. **Ask for insight, never a job.** A job ask turns a helper into an obligated party.
3. **Triage, accept the hit rate.** Silence → next name, not a better subject line.
4. **Warm before cold.** Exhaust people who already know you; cold is the last resort.
5. **The follow-up is the network.** Thank-you within 24h + periodic update notes turn one call into a
   standing relationship — the cheapest unclaimed value in a search.
6. **Networking doesn't close the loop alone.** Pair conversations with targeted applications.

## Warmth rule (resolves where the books disagree)
| | Cold contact | Warm contact |
|---|---|---|
| Strategy | Dalton volume + triage — you can't tell a helper in advance, so sample | Mann depth — invest in a short list; sampling insults an existing relationship |
| Reveal you're searching? | Oblique: signal the *category* of interest, not "I'm job hunting" | Direct: "early in my search, not asking for leads yet" (Terry) |
| Channel | LinkedIn or attested email | Text / Slack / personal email / LinkedIn |
Referral asks are **never** in the same message or meeting as a first informational; the conversation
earns a later, separate ask. Ending a meeting with *"If you were in my shoes, what would you do?"*
invites them to offer one.

## 6-Point Email (Dalton)
1. Under **75 words** 2. Ask for **insight & advice**, not job leads 3. State your **connection** first
(omit if none — never fabricate) 4. Make the request a **question** 5. Define interest **narrowly and
broadly** 6. More than half the words **about them**.

**Bracket convention** (keep visible in drafts):
- `< x >` shared-experience phrase — same phrase in subject AND body, lowercase, descriptive, not a job title
  (e.g. `< product management experience at Waymo >`)
- `<< x >>` your real connection to the reader (e.g. `<< a fellow Duke MBA >>`) — drop the sentence if none
- `<<< x >>>` category of your interest — "tell them without telling them" (e.g. `<<< product management in autonomous vehicles >>>`)

## Contact segments (Dalton)
**Boosters** help because they enjoy it — invest here. **Obligates** do the minimum and burn time before
revealing they won't help. **Curmudgeons** never reply. Dalton's follow-up cadence: no reply in
3 business days → try a second contact at that employer; still nothing at 7 → one gentle follow-up.

## TIARA (Dalton) — informational meeting questions
**T**rends · **I**nsights · **A**dvice · **R**esources · **A**ssignments. Open with Trends (easy), end on
Assignments ("What should my next step be?" / "Who else should I talk to?").

## Golden 20 (Mann)
List 20 people scored on **helpfulness × influence**; favour weak ties (new information). Tier 1 = high/high.
Used for event people-tiering too. "60 Seconds of Value": give something useful before you ask.

## LAMP (Dalton) — which employers to network into
**L**ist 40+ employers → **A**dvocacy (do you know anyone? 0–3) → **M**otivation (1–3) → **P**osting
(relevant openings? 0–3). Sort A↓, M↓, P↓; network into the top 5–10. Time-boxed: ~70 min total.

## Referral routes (Linked)
1st-degree ask (send job link + resume, mention the referral bonus) · 2nd-degree via intro · alumni ·
former colleagues · groups/communities · cold (last resort). If you know someone, ask right away; if not,
apply within ~96 hours of posting AND seek a referral in parallel.

## Listening tour (Terry) — for early-stage searches
Reverse exit interview (former colleagues: what did I do well / could do better / what would you do in my
shoes?) and the Golden Question ("If you were in my shoes, how would you approach this search?" — then
stay quiet). **Live only** (phone/video/in person). Share your two-pager for feedback.

## Triage — turning a list into a send queue
1. Dedupe against the data store; drop anyone contacted in the last 30 days unless they replied.
2. Score: Warmth (Warm 3 · Lukewarm 2 · Cold 1) + Advocacy at a target company (0–3) + Hook quality
   (specific shared experience 2 · generic 0) + Segment (Booster +1, Curmudgeon −3).
3. Honour the list's own sequencing notes if it has any (e.g. "Tier 1 first, don't blast Tier 3").
4. Output top N (default 3) with the reason and hook for each, plus a stop rule ("send these 3, log them,
   stop"). Remaining names stay queued, not drafted.

## Hard numbers
| Thing | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 6-Point Email | < 75 words, >50% about them | 2HJS |
| LinkedIn connection note | ≤ 300 characters | platform |
| Thank-you | within 24 hours | RtS, NSA |
| Update note to everyone you've spoken to | monthly–quarterly | NSA |
| Informational meeting | ask for 20–30 min; ~45 min incl. prep + follow-up | 2HJS |

## Failure modes (rank-ordered) → fixes
1. Drafted, never sent → send what exists before drafting more; ≤3 drafts per sitting; name a send-by moment.
2. No follow-up after a good conversation → thank-you task auto-created for +1 day.
3. Warm list idle while cold outreach gets worked → triage warm first.
4. Single-threading one employer → keep ≥3 active companies in the pipeline.
