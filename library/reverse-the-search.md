# Introduction

**Job Shopping** is flipping the dynamic of your job search to get companies competing for you. Job Shoppers focus their job search strategy on a specific type of opportunity and then make themselves irresistible to companies hiring for that role. This allows them to attract opportunities, land more offers, and even have companies create roles for them. The whole Job Shopping approach unlocks the power job seekers have … to be the most desirable candidate and have their pick of offers.”

2 major barriers standing in the way for professionals when it comes to taking the leap of faith and fully embracing Job Shopping

- First, it’s recognizing that there is a need for change. We tend to talk ourselves into staying at a job we don’t like. It’s the devil we know.  
- The second barrier is knowing where to start. We resort back to the easy things we know \--fiddle with our resume, send out applications, wait, eventually land an interview, end up with a single offer, take it, end up not liking it, and within six months are dreaming of being somewhere else.”

# 🤥 Chapter 1: The Myth of Job Seeking

# Chapter 1: The Myth of Job Seeking

## Myth 1: “It will be easier if I aim lower.”

Landing a position lower than your level is very challenging to do. That’s because companies are more afraid of hiring someone overqualified than they are of hiring someone who doesn’t quite have all the qualifications but could learn on the job…. They are looking for someone to do *this* job, not for someone to get their foot in the door and then ask for a promotion or salary increase within months of joining.

So instead, they’ll extend an offer to someone who meets 70 percent of the qualifications but who brings a unique combination of skills from previous jobs and is enthusiastic to learn the role.”

## Myth 2: “I need to get another degree or certification to stand out”

The thing about formal education is that it’s a ‘check the box’ qualification. If a company values ane expects a certain degree of certification, it is likely a baseline qualification that most of your competition will have as well. 

- Getting more education can be a fancy way of procrastinating.

A professional may feel they need additional education … because they are at a loss for what else to do. The answer is actually surprisingly simple: build experience and relationships. … Experience and relationships are the things that hiring managers actually value, not the extra diplomas gathering dust on your wall.

## Myth 3: “the job search is a numbers game.”

When I asked recruiters at top tech companies how many of their hires come from online applications, they reported hiring only 10 to 15 percent of their workforce from the source.  
… if only 10 percent of roles are filled by applying online, then applying online should obe only 10 percent of your strategy.   
One of the key attributes of Job Shoppers is their focus on a low-volume job search … Instead you focus on a small number that actually match what you are looking for.

## Myth 4: “I need to stay open to many different types of roles so I don’t miss out on potential opportunities.”

Unfortunately if you brand yourself to employers as a human Swiss Army knife, they will assume you are good at many things but great at nothing, and will not hire you to solve their specific challenges. 

- Essentially, showing that you’re open to many different roles sends up major red flags to employers that you will be difficult to retrain.”

## Myth 5: Things will eventually become clear, and it will all work out.

A common misstep is that job seekers make is they fall in love with the idea of a company but haven’t done the due diligence of understanding what the day-to-day would look like in that job.

Life is too short to do something that doesn’t light you up. Even if you don’t know what it is yet, you need to be relentless until you figure it out.

## Takeaways

- Myth 1: Aiming lower makes the process easier  
- Myth 2: More degrees or certifications guarantee success  
- Myth 3: The numbers game mentality  
- Myth 4: The scattered, open-to-anything approach  
- Myth 5: Job search without soul search

# Chapter 2: The Job Shopper Advantage

# ⏭️ Chapter 3: Uncovering Your Next Career Step

# Chapter 3: Uncovering Your Next Career Step

The prework of understanding who you are and what you want is the first real step of Job Shopping.

- First, it saves time and energy…. Getting focused dramatically decreases the job search time.  
- The way you convey your value and build your expertise is so impactful when you have direction. … reading the right books, attending the right events, building the right side projects…  
- Third, if your online presence and resume all convey one specific expertise, you’ll get more interviews and offers, full stop. A Job shopper zeroes in on thor next career move and then doesn’t dilute their message with a board value proposition.  
- Finally, knowing what you want will make you happier

### Stop Following your Passions

Jobs are to make money, first and foremost. … we see a **dream job** as:

- A job that energizes us  
- A paycheck and lifestyle  
- Work that feels meaningful  
- Company with people who we respect and trust

**Values \+ Strengths \+ Market Demand \= Well-th**

“A Well-thy life is about not just living rich but living well. … a Well-thy life is also one where you have the time and energy to do the things you want to do.”

## Strengths

… if you’re looking for fun, look to build mastery … being great at something and making a lot of money for it.  
Think back to the things you’ve built mastery in, and how it got especially fun as you leveled up.  
When you have a natural inclination for something, it gets more fun the better you get.

### 2\. Ask the people in your life.

- Get an outside perspective  
- Ask current and former coworkers and managers, “What are my strengths?”  
- Write down the names of five people who know your work style and reach out to them.   
- “I could use your feedback (+ego boost) . What do you see as my top strengths?”

### 3\. Do Personal Reflection

Take out a notebook and write down times when you felt most confident and successful in your work.   
Then identify what skills or abilities were responsible for that feeling.  
Write down positive feedback you have received from managers or colleagues, and look for patterns in what they have said.  
Answer these questions:

1. What information do I naturally consume?  
2. What skills do I pick up easily?  
3. What am I good at that most people dread?

Too often, people don’t go deep enough… What are the strengths that are under the surface of being a great performer? Those can often include communication, creativity, collaboration, and adaptability. aBy looking at these deeper strengths, new possibilities for career paths arise 

- Narrow down to your top 5 strengths. \- the magic number to help you realistically find a role that plays to all of them.  
- Find roles that play to your strengths, without the unrealistic expectation that your job needs to be your everything.

## Market Demand

**Market Demand** means looking at the job market and understanding, for the jobs you’re interested in:

1. How much does this career path pay at each stage of the career?  
2. Are role openings increasing or decreasing?

### 1\. How much does this career path pay at each stage?

For each career path you are considering, get an idea of what you’ll be paid early, mid, and late in that career.

- Research the average salary for that role in different companies and locations  
- Talk to people who work in the field and ask them about their pay and benefits:  
  “Given your experience in this field, how much should someone in this path expect to make at each level?”

### 2\. Are role openings increasing or decreasing?

- Stay abreast of which skills are in demand, which are being replaced by technology, and which technologies you need to become adept at using.  
- Research the number of job openings and projections for future job growth in that field  
  - [bls.gov](http://bls.gov) (Occupational Outlook Handbook)  
  - \# of openings for different titles on job boards  
  - Talk to people who are in the profession \- how hard it is to land roles, whether they see roles being in demand for the future  
- It’s important before you dive into your next role that you have a clear understanding of what salary you’ll be making and if jobs are being created in that field, rather than being removed.” p. 51

## Well-th Formula into Action

1. Establish your top 5 values  
2. Establish your top 5 strengths  
3. Create a list of possible roles you could do.  
4. Research each career path for market demand (eliminate those that immediately fail)  
5. Speak with people in each of those roles (continue eliminating options)  
6. Continue speaking w/ professionals until you’ve narrowed next career move down to the one you want.

*Be methodical \- clarity creates speed.* 

Online research → Initial Target List → Well-th Formula P/F → Informational Interviews → Refined Target List → Wel-th Formula P/F → Chosen Job

Create a list of possible roles you could do, write down the first few obvious ones, but then go deeper.

1. Use online resources \- professional networking platforms, industry forums, job search websites  
2. Keep an eye on growth sectors in industry reports and trend analyses   
3. Conduct informational interviews \- ask about roles, responsibilities, skills required  
4. Attend Networking events, conferences, meetings in various fields

You must speak to people in those jobs to understand what it would be like.

- What they do all day, the worst parts of their jobs, how much you should expect to earn at each stage

- Don’t stop until you have clarity

Job Shoppers stay curious at each career move. They revisit their Well-th Formula and never stop doing informational interviews.

- This is what will unlock career happiness and a Well-thy life. The candidates that can articulate what they want out of a role, clearly understand their strengths, and know that the job is in demand come across as much more confident and self--assured in the interview process. Time after time, these candidates are the ones who get hired.”

## Takeaways

- **Well-th Formula: Values \+ Strengths \+ Market Demand \= Well-th**

# 📜 Chapter 4: Your Resume Isn’t about You

# Chapter 4: Your Resume Isn’t about You

Frankly, companies aren’t all that interested in your previous careers. What they do want to know is if you have the ability to rock the job they’re hiring for. They also don’t want ot hear a vague list of talents and soft skills. Instead, they want to know that even if you‘re a career changer, you have prepared yourself to do *this* role, and that you can articulate exactly how you have the skills and can meet those goals. 

Make your resume a sales page

Autobiography syndrome \- Symptoms include telling your full career story to companies, including too man y past roles on your resume, and struggling to limit your resume to one page.

- … while those experiences have value, they are distracting when it comes to selling yourself to a hiring team.  
- There is an element of mourning your past career work, those underutilized degrees, the skills you spent so much time mastering, and the reputation you built--that now all needs to be sidelined, since they are not the focus of the roles you are pursuing.

### Speak to What the Company is looking for

- …the key to transforming your Wikipedia page into a sales page is starting with what a company is asking for in the job description and determining what information to share based on that.  
- When reviewing resumes, recruiters and hiring managers glance over the document quickly.  
  - They start at the top, looking at your **name** and skimming over your **summary**.  
  - Then shoot down the left  side of the page .. **job titles** and **company names**  
  - Then pause quickly to notice **education** at the bottom  
  - If you grab their attention (in \<few seconds), they will begin to read the content  
  - Looking at **summary** section, then **first** & possibly **second bullet**  of your past few jobs

Find the GLORY in your Story

## GLORY Formula 

- [Gather **Keywords**](#g-gather-keywords)  
- [List **Tasks** Performed](#l-list-tasks-performed)  
- [Observe Your **Story**](#o-observe-your-story)  
- [Refine the **Accomplishment**](#r-refine-the-accomplishment)  
- [**Yes** Statements](#y-yes-statements)

First, find 3-5 job descriptions that are representative of the job you’re pursuing \- extract skills & requirements 

## G-Gather Keywords {#g-gather-keywords}

Read the job descriptions collected; highlight terms that are specific to the profession \- hard skills

- **Hard Skills** \- specific technical or specialized knowledge and abilities that are measurable and teachable. (project management, financial modeling, etc.)  
- **Soft Skills** \[don’t include\] \- personal attributes, traits, or qualities that are not easily measurable, and are more related to interpersonal and social abilities.(communication, critical thinking, problem-solving)  
- **Keywords** \- specific enough to be relevant for small subset of jobs; they make it clear you are qualified.   
  - Pull out only the role-specific keywords and list them in a document or notebook.

## L-List Tasks Performed {#l-list-tasks-performed}

Divide a sheet of paper into two columns:

- Left column: **keywords** gathered from job description  
- Right column \- **tasks** you’ve done for most recent job that have any overlap

| Keywords (from role’s Job Description) | Task (from work experience) |
| :---- | :---- |
| Event planning | Planned quarterly offsites |
| Vendor negotiation | Negotiated w/ event and office vendors |
| Logistics coordination | Managed travel & lodging accommodations |

…narrowing the focus of your own experience to only what the company has specifically asked for. 

- **Relevance** \> Frequency  
  “It’s going to sting to set these big, juicy accomplishments aside in the name of showing the company you are exactly the square peg for their square hole…”  
- Skill gaps \- Go through each of your past roles one by one, listing the tasks performed that fit your keywords.   
- Do this for the last two roles you’ve held, as those roles will get disproportionately more attention than the rest of your experience.   
- If earlier roles don’t address keywords; leave that experience off entirely (or list w/o bullets)

## O-Observe Your Story {#o-observe-your-story}

“This process takes a lot of reflection …

1. First pick one of the tasks that you listed in the second step of the GLORY formula  
   1. One that gives you the clearest feeling that you accomplished something.”  
2. Ask set of questions below for that task  
   1. **Previous state:** What were things like before you did this task? How much worse were things?  
   2. **Consequence:** What would have been the consequence of not accomplishing this?  
   3. **Journey/effort:**What was the journey to completing this task? How much effort did it take?  
   4. **Leadership:**How much of it did you control and make happen? What aspects of it did you lead?  
   5. **Results/Impact:** What were the results? What was the impact of those results?  
3. Pick another one on the list, repeat.

- Gather:Historical facts \- files, papers, social posts, emails, former colleagues etc. to spark memory  
- Work on GLORY Formula over a multiple days \- mind primed, memory triggered by day-to-day  
- Give yourself grace \- Idea / imperfect memory \> perfect precision

## R-Refine the Accomplishment {#r-refine-the-accomplishment}

#### **Accomplishments \> Responsibilities**

- **Responsibilities** \- tasks/duties that you were assigned or expected to perform in a role or position  
  - Day-to-day activities and expectations related to a specific position.  
  - Don’t provide any  indication of whether you were good at what you were responsible for  
- **Accomplishment** \- highlights achievements, results, or contributions you have made in your past toles  
  - Demonstrate how effectively you performed your responsibilities and the impact you had on the organization or team.   
  - Showcases your skills, abil/ities, and the value you brought to your previous employers.

#### **Action verb \+ Keywords \+ Quantification**

- **Action verbs** \- strong & specific words that convey a sense of achievement and highlight your active role in completing a task or achieving a goal (achieved, resolved, generated, managed, initiated)  
- **Keywords** \- relevant terms or phrases specific to your industry or the job you are applying for.  
- **Quantification** \- adding specific numbers, metrics, percentages, or other quantifiable measures to describe the scope & impact of your accomplishments \- tangible evidence of your success & demonstrate the value you brought. Use estimations when needed.

| Type | Description | Example |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Money | Budget, revenue, cost | Annual budget of $200k |
| Time | Length or amount | 9-month long project |
| Amount | People, tasks, etc. | Wrote 10+ articles |
| Frequency | Per day, week, month | Scheduled 40-50 mtgs/week |
| Growth | $, volume, % increase | 33% increase in satisfaction |
| Reduction | $, volume, % decrease | Streamlined process from 12 → 4 steps |
| Scale | How many? How big? | 120 attendees |


  


  

- Be specific, concise, and results-oriented when crafting accomplishment statements.

## Y-Yes Statements {#y-yes-statements}

- Statements placed in a highlights section at the top of your resume, or in your experience section, that allow a hiring manager to skim the paper in 30s and understand why they should hire you.  
- Tailored specifically to the role you’ve applied to; they are the ones that will make the hiring manager put your resume in the ‘yes’pile to move forward in the process.

Placed in your resume to:

- Draw attention to specific expertise  
- Focus on older but still relevant accomplishments  
- Translate relevance of past role to target role.

Helpful for career pivots, older jobs

Yes Statements \- essentially summaries that call out important keywords so that the person reading your resume quickly understands that you have the correct skills and knowledge.

- A bullet at the top of the resume in a highlights or summary section:

***“\_\_\_\_ professional with experience in \_\_\_, \_\_\_, and \_\_\_ in the \_\_\_ industry.”***

- 1st blank: translate the type of professional you are (someone who has the skills and knowledge to do a job). Find the commonality between your current job and the next job.  
- Next 3: Keywords for the role \- top 3 keywords from Gather Keywords stage  
- Industry \- include if in line w/ role; otherwise leave out

Placement:

- **Highlights** section \- small section at the top of your resume containing yes statements and other notable information for resume reader.  
  - Fill in rest of section w/ important certifications \+ relevant past accomplishments  
  - “Write … in such a way that even if the reader doesn’t go beyond that first section, they will already know you’re qualified.”  
- Atop each **job experience** \- brief summary of what important tasks performed that fulfill the keywords.

…predictability makes for an effortless reading experience for the reader. … for Job Shoppers … our goal must be to provide the reader with only the most relevant details, and to make it as clear as possible, as quickly as possible, that you are the right person for the job. 

## Takeaways

- Tailor application to **company’s needs \> own story,** including every detail of career history  
- Follow **GLORY Formula**: targeted resume that resonates with hiring managers.  
  - **Gather Keywords** \- find hard skills in job descriptions  
  - **List Tasks Performed** \- Match what you’ve done in previous jobs to hard skills in JDs  
  - **Observe Your Story** \- Recognize value & impact in tasks performed, challenges overcome, results achieved  
  - **Refine the Accomplishment** \- on resume: Action Verb \+ Keywords \+ Quantification  
  - **Yes Statements** \- place strategically at top of resume, in experience sections to highlight tailored relevant skills, experience, accomplishments

# Chapter 5: Build Your Brand and the Jobs Will Find You

**Sourcing** \- enables companies to proactively search for individuals with specific experience and attract them to consider the job openings. … Moreover, sourcing can help companies gain a competitive advantage in the hiring process. 

- By engaging in sourcing, companies can reach out to potential candidates before they have a chance to apply elsewhere, increasing the likelihood of securing highly qualified individuals. Sourcing allows companies to be proactive and assertive in their recruitment efforts. 

## Building your online brand to get sourced

- **Value Add \> Prev. Experience:** focus online branding around value you’ll add, not where you’ve been

Ideal profile

- Headline \- clearly states target profession & industry  
- Work samples & summaries revolve around chosen profession  
  - Where your career is going  
  - Why you are excited about it  
  - What experience from your past sets you up perfectly to succeed in it

Review online presence \- *Is it immediately clear what job a company should hire you for?*

- Networking sites, portfolio site, etc.  
- Make public (check privacy settings)

### Step 2: Put the right keywords on your profile

- Most important part to branding is having the right keywords associated with your expertise  
- Job titles \- similar or related to the role they are hiring for  
- Translate your internal job title to be something more accurate or recognizable.  
- Skills & Keywords associated with the profession  
  - Focus on the top 5 keywords from FLORY exercise  
- By combining your previous top 5 keywords with the title keywords, you can create a new top 5 keyword list for your LinkedIn page.”  
  - Top oof LI profile, resume, personal website.  
- In other parts of profile \- add additional relevant keywords  
  - Role-relevant  
  - Industry-specific  
- Be clear about type of company  and industry … because you appear as a perfect fit to employers in that industry.   
  - Career pivoting: focus on the role you want in the future, not the roles you’ve had in the past”

### Step 3: Grow Your Network

- Having a wide pool of connections is a key component of online branding. …  10+ yrs, \>2k connections  
- First, list all the organizations you have been a part of … add them (vendors, contractors, facilitators, etc.)  
  -   
- Add strangers into the mix … find people in your industry and/or field and connect on a bunch.   
  - Add a customized connection message for someone you’d particularly like to connect with:

### Bonus-Step 4: Create Content

- Creating content & building thought leadership \- use your voice: Posts, articles, podcasts  
- **Quality comments**  \- on the work of other individuals … share what you took away from it, as well as other observations you have.

- **Content creation** \- powerful tool to enhance online presence, through leadership, engage w/ industry peers & showcase expertise

## The 90-day Plan

4. Interviewer will be picturing you in the role. 

Should contain:

- Overarching **Goals**  
- Key **Deliverables** for those goals  
- Rough **Timeline** for those deliverables (by week or month)

**Create a 90 day plan**

2. **Inquire**: ask questions in interviews to deeply understand the current state of the business, how this role fits, what this job is expected to accomplish

   1. 3rd round interview or later  
   2. If interview is over, offer to email to hiring manager

# 💱 Chapter 9: Negotiation is a Partnership

# Chapter 9: Negotiation is a Partnership

Collaborative negotiation approach: continue building relationship with the hiring manager.

Many people don’t negotiate their job offer, for several reasons.

- First, there is a fear that the company may take the offer away or that the candidate may appear ungrateful.  
- Second, there’s  a bit of confusion as to what are the “rules.”  
- Finally, if the person is happy with the offer they’ve received, they’re afraid to push for more if it could potentially sour the relationship.

I still encourage my clients to negotiate. There are two main reasons:

- One, the company expects you to negotiate. … even if it’s at the top of the posted salary range.  
- Two, if they extend an offer and you accept immediately … This sets the tone for you to get fewer or lower salary increases form the company in the future.”

***“Which aspects of this offer are negotiable, if any?”***

- Not so much what you ask for but how you ask for it that will ensure the negotiation goes smoothly. 

## Navigating the “Salary Question” early in the process

- **The first person who names a number loses**. Avoid giving a number if at all possible.  
  - Candidates who wait until the end to negotiate on average get bigger increases to their offers.   
    - Hiring team is sold on you; wants to end hiring process.  
  - You have the power … Additionally, when you make asks in the negotiation, you’re doing so from a position of the company better understanding your value.”  
  - “Flexible” \- do your research on the role.   
- “What are your salary expectations?”  
  - ***“I’m open, looking for the best fit and overall package.”***  
- A good offer is more than a base salary, it’s a combination of everything tangible and intangible that matters to you … keep what you’re shopping for in mind and know what you value most.  
- “What range of compensation are you looking for?”  
  - ***“Do you have a range that you’re targeting so I can let you know if I’m comfortable with it?”***  
- **Do your research ahead of time** \- it pays off  
  - How much the company pays for the job you’re interviewing for  
  - Research comparable companies: same industry, same city, similar size  
- **Aim for numbers higher in the range:**  
  - Bottom of range: somewhere between 50th and 80th percentile of researched ranges  
    - Make sure lowest number is one you would be excited to accept.  
  - Top of range: aligned with top found during salary research

Upon insistence, based on research:

- ***…looking at different salary ranges for companies like yours in your city for this type of role. What I found was a range between $176k and $197k. Does that fall in your range?”***  
  - I could see how that \[lower end\] salary could be a piece of a great overall package

1. Avoid applying online \- use referrals  
2. Skip the question  or put a well-researched range  
3. In interview: I’m open, looking for best fit & overall package  
   1. Do you have a target range, so I can let you know if i’m comfortable?  
   2. What I saw from similar companies for this role…

## Negotiating at the Offer Stage

“Researchers found that the first five minutes of the sociometric data strongly predicted the outcome of the negotiation. Put another way, it was the rapport the negotiator built and the impression they made that allowed them to get a better deal in the end.”

**3 major elements**

1. #### **Gratitude** 

- show gratitude for the initial offer and any adjustment; express interest in the role.

2. #### **Clarification**

- ask questions to understand how offer is crafted so you can have mutual understanding.  
  1. What skills / experience differentiate a manager from a senior manager?  
  2. Could you clarify how performance based bonuses are structured, criteria for eligibility  
  3. Details about the equity component of the offer?

3. #### **Acknowledgement**

- acknowledge where company is coming from, limitations, accommodations  
  1. Active listening  
  2. Be flexible and open to compromise: demonstrate adaptability and willingness to work together toward a mutually beneficial outcome.

“Practicing active listening, being flexible, and demonstrating a willingness to collaborate contribute to a constructive negotiation process where both parties can find common ground and work toward an outcome that benefits both.”

#### **Negotiation Steps**

1. Show gratitude  
2. Get full details of the offer  
3. Let them know you want to take a moment to consider it  
4. Ask for additional information about offer (i.e., healthcare, vacation, etc.)  
5. Challenge yourself to ask for more, even if market rate  
   1. ***“Based on my conversations with companies and the salaries I am seeing in the market, I am targeting a salary of $\_\_\_. Is there a way to make up the difference?”***  
6. Choose an unusual number (158,500 \> 155,000 gives impression of deeper research).  
7. Make the middle slightly higher, you may get higher offer  
8. Be prepared to back up salary amount you’re asking for if necessary.  
   1. Another company, research, your qualifications & unique expertise

In negotiations, it’s important to say less.  
180,000 (company’s offer) → | (negotiated offer likely in this range) \- 190k \- | → $200k (your salary target)

#### **What to negotiate besides salary**

- Title  
- Sign-on Bonus  
- Severance  
- Stock Options  
- Office Setup  
- Computer  
- Relocation assistance  
- Flexible schedule  
- Performance Review (3/6 month review into contract to re-evaluate comp based on performance.)

**“Thank you for discussing the offer with me. I intend to sign, and I look forward to working together\!”**

If you decline, do so respectfully \- give a reason that is a bit out of their control. 

“Nothing wrong with getting to end of hiring process and saying no … best approach is to be open-minded in the interview process, see opportunities through to the end, and utilize the fact that you are in final rounds with a company to speed up other interview processes, and come out with multiple offers where you are making a thoughtful decision.”

## Secure the Best Overall Offer

Building rapport & maintaining a positive relationship with the company throughout the process is crucial. 

- It’s about securing a package that aligns with your values and long-term career goals.

## Takeaways

- After an offer, pause, **express gratitude** & interest in *potentially* working together  
- Don’t feel pressured to give an answer in the moment \- ask additional questions about offer  
- **Be Direct & succinct** when negotiating \- target salary above offer; see if they are open to raising  
- Don’t speak after that statement; say as little as possible throughout.  
- Continue discussing salary further or explore other aspects of the offer  
- Accept/decline w/ grace & appreciation

## Don’t Depend on Job Security

The bottom line is, there’s no such thing as job security anymore. Financial hardships hit, roles become redundant, and projects change. No matter how “loyal” you are, if a company hits poor financial times, they don’t care if you gave the CEO your kidney, you’re out of there.

It’s important to face the music when open roles in your path year over year are declining instead of increasing.

- As a job shopper, accept that life isn’t fair, then manage whatever is actually in your control.  
- You don’t chase job security, you seek career security

## Focus on Career Security

**Career Security** means you have set yourself up to attract opportunities, so that if you lose your job, have to move cities for family reasons, or need higher cash flow, you’ll have options to make an optimal career move.

- With career security you are ready to rumble should you ever need to hit the ejector switch on your current employment.   
- …recruiters regularly reaching out, network easy to reactivate, tracking achievements to prepare for future resumes and interviews

## Build Career Security in the Good Times

- “Just as we shouldn’t go grocery shopping when we are hungry, it’s also best to not start Job Shopping when desperate."… by actively maintaining your Job Shopper status, in good times and in bad. By continuing to flex your jJob Shopper muscles through the ups and downs of your career, you'll still have assets like your personal brand, marketable skills, and relationships, even if you were to lose your job tomorrow.”

## Steps to Take to Achieve Career Security

### Track Accomplishments {#track-accomplishments}

1) Take note of **current benchmarks** in new role \- data points or status of projects before you’ve started  
2) Make note of the **“before” picture** for anything you’ll be working on (use for raise/promotion)  
3) Keep an **accomplishments log** \- a document that tracks a lot of the smaller projects or hard work

### Build Relationships Internally {#build-relationships-internally}

- Intentional focus on meaningful relationships each week  
- …what is so brilliant about building relationships is that every single person you get to know who … becomes a potential referral to whatever company they work for next.   
- *If you want Career Security, take a genuine interest in people.*  
- Fill your work calendar with 15-minutes check-ins… every 6 weeks, quarterly. Be intentional about keeping contact.

### Get Clear on Your Next Career Step {#get-clear-on-your-next-career-step}

- Consistently ask yourself, ***“What is my next career step?”*** through your tenure at a job.  
  - “Get clear with yourself \- the easiest way to … to land the exact opportunity you want next is to use your current employer to train you for that next opportunity.”  
1) Find **job descriptions** of what you’d like to do next  
2) Look at the **skill gap** between where you are now and what the other role requires  
3) **Raise your hand for projects** at current company that could close those gaps  
- **Career Pivots** \- “do your best to do most of the pivoting while still at the same company. It is much easier to pivot within a company than to…try to get another business to take a chance on your pivot.  
- “...really the thing that opens doors is clarity and the ability to articulate what you want.”  
- After a few months in, ask  
  - What do I want next? Same thing, higher level? Other areas of the business? Wrong place?

### Keep Your Online Brand Up to Date and Your Network Warm {#keep-your-online-brand-up-to-date-and-your-network-warm}

- **Brand refresh:** Put a monthly event on your calendar, or every 6-8 weeks, use that time to adjust anything that’s outdated on your profile, add any new professional contacts to LinkedIn network, and reach out to 5 people you haven’t talked to in a little while  
- **Networking at Scale:** Create content regularly on professional networking social media platforms, career focused forums, chat groups, or blog posts

## Become a Job Shopper at Your New Job

- Embrace Career Security \- forget about clinging to a job at a single company:  
  - Cultivate skills  
  - Nurture professional relationships  
  - Craft a robust personal brand  
- Career security isn’t a set-it-and-forget-it approach to your career; it’s a lifelong commitment to staying relevant and resilient in the face of change.

## Takeaways

- **Career Security \> job security** (myth) … prepared for job loss or transition
